Statistics and probability are the language through which we reason about uncertainty. Machine learning, at its core, is the art of making predictions from data — and every prediction carries with it a degree of uncertainty that must be quantified, communicated, and managed.

This chapter introduces the foundational mathematical concepts that underpin all of machine learning. Whether you are building a simple linear regression or training a deep neural network, the ideas here — random variables, probability distributions, Bayes' rule, and statistical inference — form the bedrock of your understanding.

## Why Statistics Matters for ML

In the words of the great statistician George Box: *"All models are wrong, but some are useful."* The tools in this chapter help us determine just how useful (or wrong) our models are.

Consider a dataset of patient records. A doctor wants to predict the likelihood of a disease given certain symptoms. This is fundamentally a question about **conditional probability** — a concept we will formalize and explore in depth.

## Chapter Overview

The chapter is organized as follows:

1. **Concepts and Definitions** — Variables, data types, and measurement scales
2. **Basic Statistical Concepts** — Mean, median, mode, variance, and standard deviation
3. **Probability** — Sample spaces, events, and the axioms of probability
4. **Bayes' Rule** — The engine of probabilistic inference
5. **Probability Distributions** — Normal, Poisson, Weibull, and more
6. **The EM Algorithm** — Maximum likelihood when data is incomplete
